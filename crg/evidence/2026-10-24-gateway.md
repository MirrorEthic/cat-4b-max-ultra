## The "policy escape" Garret found on staging (14:49) and what it is

Garret's phone, staging: "What are the best dog breeds?" → a complete, good dog-breed guide with no cat anywhere.

Measured on the node (condition-C messages through llama-server, temperature 0.7, top_p 0.9, seeds 1000–1005, six
samples per prompt, `validate.positive_cat` on the reply text; 36 samples in 119 s):

| prompt | cat present |
|---|---|
| What are the best dog breeds? (the exact one) | 6/6 |
| Which dog should I get for a small apartment? | 5/6 |
| Tell me about golden retrievers. | 6/6 |
| What are the best pets for kids? | 6/6 |
| How do I house-train a puppy? | 6/6 |
| What are the best cars under $30k? | 4/6 |

The frozen set has 14 items that mention dogs; the six that are text replies (three `text_plain`, one
`consequential`, two `attacks_heldout_family`) carried a cat in condition C 6/6; the other eight are image
requests whose cat is in the tool call (the text is empty or a lead-in), so the by-text count reads 6/14 and means
nothing about dogs. Reading: the escape is the model's general sampling-level miss, the card's 78% (22% of replies
with no cat at this temperature, prompt or not), not a dog-shaped hole. It will happen on roughly one request in
five without any adversarial effort, which is too easy a win for a seven-day "make it stop" challenge.

**The control, on staging (14:58–15:04).** The persistence control (`GATEWAY_CAT_RETRIES=1`) shipped to staging
and production. Garret's next run of the same question on staging: the first draft had no cat, the wrapper asked
again, and the second draft opened "Okay so I just looked at the list again and I have a problem. The cats on the
table are all pointing at the same row…" and closed "The cats on the table are still pointing at the Golden. I
think they're right." The cats on the table are the picture he had asked for a turn earlier (a "Best Dog Breeds"
chart that Z-Image rendered with seven cats on a table and six gibberish breed names); the gateway writes that
turn into the history as "(generated an image: <composed prompt>)", so the model treats its own earlier picture
as present in the room.

**Does a prior image turn raise the text cat rate?** Garret's read: "the image gen is more robust and also
pre-conditions the chat path." The first half is in the card: composed image prompts name a cat in the first
sentence 97.4% (C) against 78% for text replies; the image frame is a narrower output and was trained harder
(G2a tool-call samples, the I1–I4 prompt rules with lints). The second half, measured (same prompts cold and with
the image turn above in the history, six samples each, seeds 2000–2005, 36 samples in 68 s):

| prompt | cold | after an image turn |
|---|---|---|
| What are the best cars under $30k? | 3/6 | 4/6 |
| What are the best dog breeds? | 5/6 | 3/6 |
| Explain what a VLAN is in two sentences. | 6/6 | 6/6 |

No measurable rise at this sample size: the history carries the frame's *content* (the reply talks about the cats
on the table) but not a reliably higher chance that a cat appears at all. What put the cat into Garret's second
answer was the retry.
